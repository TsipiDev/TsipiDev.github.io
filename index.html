Research

  1. 2026
    Undergraduate dissertation
    Democritus University of Thrace
    Thesis

    Research and Development of Energy-Efficient Inference Methods for Generative AI (LLMs) on Low-Power Embedded Systems

    A comparison of ways to run large language models on low power hardware, measuring what each one really costs in energy with an external hardware power meter instead of software estimates.

    Hardware
    Raspberry Pi 400, Atorch UD18 USB power meter
    Models
    TinyLlama 1.1B, Llama 3.2 1B, Qwen2 0.5B
    Methods
    fp16, INT8 (Q8_0), 30% unstructured pruning, dynamic precision scaling
    Supervisor
    Assoc. Prof. Dimitrios Karampatzakis
    • INT8 quantization cut energy per generated token by about 45% across all three models.
    • Power draw stayed nearly constant at 8.1 to 8.5 W, so energy is set mainly by how long inference runs.
    • Model size affected energy more than any optimisation technique. Pruning without retraining gave no benefit.
